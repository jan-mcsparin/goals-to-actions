# Goals to Actions: one-page concept

Draft v1, 2026-10-26; brought in line with the stage 3 decisions on 2026-11-09. Built first for me, as practice and as a tool I'll use daily. An installable, mobile-first web app.

## The problem

Even when I know what I want and roughly what it takes, I stall, sometimes for years or even fail outright.  Due to present bias, what feels easy or comfortable right now wins over what I want far more for the future.  My to-do lists capture today's tasks but not the big themes, so I can't tell which tasks actually move me forward. And a plan that ignores the work of keeping life running, or leaves no time to rest, falls apart.

## The promise

Tell it what you want. It plans your days so life keeps running, the right tasks move you forward, and there's still time to enjoy it. It keeps you connected to why and shows how you're progressing so you don't lose momentum and either stay on track or adjust. Your energy each day goes into doing.

**The aim:** not a short, grandiose burst of progress, but a life where I keep moving forward and improving, over years, while also being happy. Success is measured over months and years, not weeks.

## The model

- **Aspiration:** who I want to be, with the why behind it ("Be fit, so I can keep skiing, biking and playing hockey at a high level for decades").
- **Goal:** a measurable definition of that, keeping my own label ("Be strong: bench 175, squat 350, deadlift 400"). One aspiration usually has several goals: "Be fit" could also include "Be fast: run a 7-minute mile."
- **Milestones:** benchmarks that prove I'm improving, starting from where I am now (bench 145, squat 275, deadlift 315).
  - Week 8: bench 155, squat 300, deadlift 345
  - Week 16: bench 165, squat 325, deadlift 375
  - Week 24: bench 175, squat 350, deadlift 400 (goal)
- **Actions:** the tasks that move me from one milestone to the next.
  - Weekly target: 3 lifting sessions (squat day, bench day, deadlift day), on whichever days fit
  - Add 5 lb to a lift once I complete all its sets and reps
  - Test my maxes at each milestone

Goal types shape the actions: **building** goals get flexible weekly targets, **quitting** goals get a start date and daily all-or-nothing tracking, and **one-off** goals get a deadline and a short checklist.

## A balanced day

Every plan balances three kinds of time, from the start:

- **Keeping life running:** the things that have to get done (laundry, cooking, paying bills). Recurring, set up once, and only the ones that actually slip.
- **Moving forward:** the actions toward goals.
- **Rest:** free time to relax and enjoy life, protected rather than leftover.

Maintenance goes in first, goal work fits into what's left, and the day is never packed full. Without the first, there's no room to move forward; without the last, none of it is sustainable.

## The core loop (what the prototype does)

1. **Name an aspiration:** the underlying motivation, why I want what I want ("Be fit, so I can keep skiing, biking and playing hockey at a high level for decades"). Changes rarely.
2. **Set a goal** that defines concretely what reaching that aspiration means to me, from one sentence. One aspiration can have several goals. I set the milestones and weekly targets myself in the prototype; app suggestions are a nice-to-have for later.
3. **Task bank:** each week's tasks sit in a bank: goal tasks (generated or added by hand) and recurring life maintenance.
4. **Proposed daily plan:** the app drafts the week, fitting maintenance first, then goal work, and leaving free time, and keeps it updated as the week goes, so I can look ahead anytime. I accept, swap or skip. A plan exists even if I never open it.
5. **Do and check off,** with each task showing the goal it serves and the aspiration behind it.
6. **See progress:** pacing ("2 of 3 runs, 3 days left"), milestones hit, and improvement, not just effort.
7. **Rollovers and reviews:** unfinished tasks roll over with no penalty, with a gentle "still matter?" after two weeks. Reviews happen whenever I want them; the app doesn't prompt.

## Principles that shape every screen

- **Plan quality first, then minimal planning time.** Time saved goes into doing.
- **Automate what happens most often first.** Daily plans and weekly targets come before rarer steps like suggesting milestones.
- **Effort early, ease later.** Deeper setup while motivation is high; upkeep near zero after that.
- **No shame.** Missed weeks are expected. Falling short is "not yet," leading with what was achieved.
- **Ask, don't direct.** Deadlines are optional; when one slips, ask whether to push it, recommit or rethink.
- **Sensible targets.** AI suggestions stay realistic (no sudden jumps in training load).

## Out of scope for the prototype

Native iOS, other users and sign-up, values exercises, AI-suggested milestones, calendar and watch integrations, and the full safety principles (deferred until anyone else uses it).

## How I'll know it works

- I accept most proposed plans with little editing, and spend only a few minutes a day planning.
- I hit milestones I would otherwise have drifted on.
- After several months, I'm still improving and still enjoying my life. Hard stretches and breaks are both fine; what matters is the long run.
- After a month, I'm still opening it daily, and planning touches are going down while progress goes up.
- Life's maintenance stops slipping, and most days still have real free time.
